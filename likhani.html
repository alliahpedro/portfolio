01

Overview

Giving student moving-image works a permanent, searchable home.

LikhaNi is a digital archive for Asia Pacific College's School of Multimedia Arts (SoMA). It keeps student short films, documentaries, animations and motion graphics in one searchable place instead of scattered across discs and drives.

It is a moderated institutional archive, not a streaming service. Students request to publish, administrators review, and the school keeps ownership of the work. The project began under the working name SoMAVault.

02

Problem

Student films were disappearing into old discs and drives.

For years, students submitted a printed paper with a compact disc for library storage. Discs deteriorate, formats go out of date, and files ended up on disconnected drives and temporary cloud storage. When DOST asked for the 2019 thesis film Freehand for Persons with Disability Week, the school could not provide a copy. LikhaNi exists so that does not happen again.

03

Goals

What the archive had to do.

01

Preserve Student Works

Replace discs, drives and temporary cloud storage with one centralized archive the school owns.

02

Make Work Easy to Find

Searchable metadata and structured browsing by category, genre and course.

03

Control storage and quality with a request ticket, admin review and expiring upload links.

04

A consistent brand identity and accessible interface that reads as an official school archive.

04

Research and Insights

What the surveys and testing showed.

01

Survey Findings

Two Microsoft Forms surveys, 56 respondents each, covered browsing habits, submission preferences and visual style.

68% use a laptop or desktop when looking for creative references or sharing work.

44 of 56 respondents start browsing from featured or recommended content.

Selected evaluators then tested the prototype with task-based, think-aloud sessions.

02

User Needs

Quick publication: 41% accept one to three days and 27% expect it within 24 hours.

Familiar ways to submit. Email was the most preferred method.

Several ways in: featured work, latest uploads, tags or genre, and course or subject.

A place to learn from other students' work and techniques.

03

Pain Points

Submitting by USB, MS Forms or email raised worries about security, privacy, ownership and uncontrolled access.

Respondents also mentioned file corruption and improper crediting.

68% said slow devices or data limits affect how they feel about animated sites.

Past storage on discs and disconnected drives had already lost a thesis film.

04

Key Insights

48% were fine with admin upload, 41% said it depends on turnaround and transparency, and 11% wanted direct self-publishing.

The most animated reference site won (52%), ahead of the Vimeo-style (32%) and A24-style (16%) references.

61% prefer moderate animation on sites they use regularly.

Students want both immersion and ease, so performance had to win ties.

Research Summary

Students wanted a place that feels creative but loads fast, and a submission process they can trust. Survey results and panel feedback shaped the home page hierarchy, the moderated workflow and the restrained motion.

05

Design Decisions

How the research became design.

01

Moderated Submissions

Students send a publication request first. After admin approval they receive a time-limited upload link by email, which keeps storage and files under control.

02

Featured Work First

The home page opens on a featured work, then rows by category such as Animation, Film and Documentary, matching where most respondents begin.

03

Restrained Motion

Moderate animation and clear grids instead of the heavily animated style respondents liked most, because many are on slow devices or limited data.

04

An Institutional Identity

A crimson and neutral palette, Poppins throughout, and a LikhaNi wordmark that replaced SoMAVault, so the site reads as an official school archive.

06

Solution Showcase

Five views of the solution.

01User Flow

Overview

The submission workflow turns a student's request into a published work through admin review, with a visible status at every step.

Key Highlights

  • Request first, upload later, so nothing arrives without approval.
  • Upload links expire after use or after a limited time, like password-reset links.
  • Administrators can return a request to the student for revision.
  • Students follow progress on My Submissions: Submission Received, Admin Review, Published.

Outcome

Storage stays manageable and every file passes through the school before it is published.

02Wireframes

Overview

Early layouts from the SoMAVault stage mapped the home page, browsing rows, video page and the student and admin dashboards before any visual styling.

Key Highlights

  • Started from placeholder brand guidelines and a first user flow that later needed refining.
  • Category rows with a featured area set the structure of the final home page.
  • Student and admin dashboards were planned as separate spaces from the start.
  • Moved from low-fidelity to mid-fidelity to high-fidelity, then to an interactive prototype.

Outcome

Wireframing fixed content organization and navigation placement before the visual identity was built.

03Design System

Overview

A small system keeps the archive consistent across the public site, the student pages and the admin dashboard.

Key Highlights

  • Poppins for all text, from ExtraBold 800 for major headings to Medium 500 for body.
  • Dark mode #1A1A1A, light mode #F5F5F5, and crimson #8A181A for sign-in pages.
  • Primary, secondary and tertiary buttons with 8 to 12 px radius and 12 to 16 px padding.
  • Built to WCAG and usability heuristics so it stays inclusive and predictable.

Outcome

One visual language for three audiences, and an identity that reads as institutional.

04Key Screens

Overview

The home page for browsing, My Submissions for students and a dashboard for administrators.

Key Highlights

  • Home: a featured work, Most Liked, Most Viewed and Recent tabs, and rows by category.
  • My Submissions: a progress tracker, submission details and uploaded assets.
  • Dashboard: urgent items, pending reviews, needs revision, daily submission limit and storage used.
  • The dashboard needed the most iteration because of moderation and governance requirements.

Outcome

Students can discover and submit work, and administrators can moderate it and keep the archive healthy.

07

Results and Outcomes

What testing and feedback produced.

01

Usability

  • Task completion improved after each round of testing.
  • Evaluators were less confused in later sessions.
  • Navigation labels, visual hierarchy and layout were revised after feedback.
  • Interaction behavior was made consistent across pages.
02

Feedback

  • Evaluators responded positively on usability, accessibility and overall experience.
  • The panel asked for playback resume, stronger visual alignment and clearer submission requirements.
  • Adviser and peer critiques fed each revision.
03

Impact

  • A working archive with video streaming for SoMA students and alumni.
  • Selected works from students and alumni were gathered for inclusion.
  • Launch assets were prepared for official SoMA channels.
04

Results

  • 112 survey responses from two surveys, plus task-based testing, behind the design.
  • Built to WCAG and heuristic evaluation principles.
  • Concluded as a practical, scalable model for preserving student work at APC.
08

What I Learned

What this project taught me.

01

Data can pull against taste

Most respondents preferred the most animated reference site, yet many said slow devices affect them. Performance and accessibility decided the direction.

02

Admin tools take the longest

The dashboard needed the most iteration because moderation, content management and governance all had to fit one interface.

03

Constraints shape the product

Large video files and limited storage led to a moderated workflow instead of open uploads.
