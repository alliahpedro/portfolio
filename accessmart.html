Overview

A marketplace built so people can shop independently.

5 hrs competition sprint to design a high-fidelity marketplace

AccessMart is a high-fidelity e-commerce marketplace designed in a 5-hour competition at SIKAPTala 2026, hosted by DLSU-D CICS.

It helps persons with disabilities browse and purchase mobility aids and daily living tools independently, from canes and crutches to rollators and scooters.

02

Problem

Online shopping is hard when the interface is not built for you.

Users with motor, visual, or cognitive impairments face real barriers in e-commerce. Independent shopping becomes difficult or impossible.

01

Small Tap Targets

Buttons and links that are hard to hit for users with limited motor control.

02

Low-Contrast Text

Text that is difficult to read for users with visual impairments.

03

Cluttered Checkout

Crowded flows that make it hard to finish a purchase without help.

03

Design Decisions

One answer for each barrier.

01

High-Contrast Storefront

White type on a deep navy background, with bold headlines and strong separation between content blocks, so text stays readable.

02

Large Type and Controls

Large headlines, oversized product images and big buttons such as Explore Catalog and Order Now, so targets are easy to hit.

03

Clear Category Browsing

An All Categories menu, a search bar and a row of product images for canes, crutches, rollators, walkers and scooters let users find what they need quickly.

04

A Simple Sign-In

One short form with Google and Facebook options, plus saved profiles to log in as, so returning users get in with one step.

04

Solution Showcase

Three screens from the marketplace.

01Home

Overview

The first screen shoppers see: the store name, search, cart, wishlist and account, then a large Mobility Aids hero.

Key Highlights

  • A utility bar for free shipping on orders over $50, language, FAQ and help.
  • Search, cart, wishlist and account in one row, with an All Categories menu below.
  • A bold Mobility Aids headline with an Explore Catalog button.
  • A photo of a person using a rollator at home, with a line about moving freely and living on your own terms.

Outcome

The purpose of the store and the first action are clear before the user scrolls.

02Landing Page

Overview

Mobility aids shown large and clear, from canes and crutches to rollators and scooters.

Key Highlights

  • A Next-Gen Accessibility banner above the main headline.
  • Product images on plain backgrounds, with the selected item enlarged.
  • Carousel dots show how many items there are.

Outcome

Users can recognise the product types by sight before reading any labels.

03Sign In

Overview

One step to mobility aids, daily living tools, and expert guidance.

Key Highlights

  • A single Sign in card with Google and Facebook options above the email and password fields.
  • Labels sit above each field, with a Forgot Password link and a No Account? Sign up link.
  • A Login as row of saved profiles for returning users.
  • A short message and a photo on the navy side panel explain what signing in unlocks.

Outcome

Getting back into the store takes one decision instead of a long form.
